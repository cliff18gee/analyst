Closed-Loop Medication & Pharmacy Optimization

01

Executive overview

Apex Health Network (AHN) runs 12 inpatient hospitals and 45 outpatient clinics. Its prescribing, pharmacy inventory and bedside administration depend on disconnected systems, paper charts and verbal confirmations. This capstone builds the business case for one connected, closed-loop medication and pharmacy platform that links every step from the prescription to reconciliation.

02

Current state and problem

Problem statement. Fragmented pharmacy, prescribing and medication-administration processes rely on disconnected systems, paper charts and manual confirmations. The result is medication errors, inventory blind spots, financial leakage, nursing workload, controlled-substance risk and limited analytics.

AreaCurrent stateBusiness impact
PrescribingPrescriptions written in one system with limited downstream integrationManual verification and transcription risk; pharmacy delays
Pharmacy inventoryLegacy standalone software; limited ward-level visibilityCritical stockouts, overstocking, expiry and waste
Medication administrationPaper charts and verbal confirmations, taking up to 30% of nurses’ shift timeWrong-drug and wrong-dose risk; high manual workload
Billing and reconciliationAdministration not fully connected to billingAdministered medicines may be missed in billing
Controlled substancesPaper tracking and manual checksRegulatory, audit and legal exposure
Management analyticsNo reliable integrated medication-utilisation reportingSlow decisions; limited cost visibility

Root-cause themes

  • Technology: disconnected systems, limited interoperability
  • Process: manual hand-offs and duplicated checks
  • People: workload, usability and training gaps
  • Governance and data: inconsistent standards, fragmented data

Potential impact

  • Errors and delays in verification or administration
  • Stockouts, expiry waste and avoidable nursing workload
  • Incomplete billing, weak audit trails, limited insight

06

Recommended solution

A single connected workflow from prescribing through reconciliation: enter once, verify safely, record every step, reconcile automatically.

  1. Order The prescriber enters the order in the EHR/HIS.
  2. Clinical check The pharmacist verifies dose, allergies and interactions.
  3. Stock check The system checks availability, expiry and replenishment.
  4. Dispense Pharmacy prepares and records the medication.
  5. Bedside scan Staff scan patient and medicine for right-patient checks.
  6. Administer Administration is recorded in the eMAR.
  7. Close the loop Inventory and billing update; exceptions feed dashboards.

Safety gate. Unresolved clinical alerts need authorised review before administration.

From as-is to to-be

As-is (current problem)To-be (future state)

07

Delivery approach

A hybrid Agile approach: iterative co-design inside formal clinical and technical stage gates.

Phase 1 · Months 0–12

Discover, design and pilot. Integrations, eMAR/BCMA, baseline measures, UAT and rollback proof.

Phase 2 · Months 13–24

Scale the inpatient rollout. Inventory, billing, controlled medicines and dashboards.

Phase 3 · Months 25–36

Optimise and extend. Advanced analytics and selected outpatient workflows.

Scale-up happens only after safety sign-off, user acceptance testing, training, downtime rehearsal and rollback readiness, which protects the 24/7 clinical service.

11

Recommendation and next steps

Decision requested: approve Phase 1 discovery and pilot planning, not an unconditional full rollout. The team recommends the integrated closed-loop platform, with funding released in stages and scale-up only when evidence supports it. Progress is conditional on a validated Phase 1 baseline and total-cost model, clinical safety, privacy and architecture sign-off before the pilot, successful UAT, training and downtime testing before scale-up, and named benefit owners for every KPI.
