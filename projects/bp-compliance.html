Improving Lying and Standing Blood Pressure Measurement Compliance

A multi-ward hospital improvement initiative designed to standardise blood pressure measurement practice and strengthen compliance with clinical guidance.

OrganisationHinchingbrooke Hospital
ScopeFive medical wards (approx. 150 inpatient beds, per the project description)
WardsCherry Tree, Plum Tree, Apple Tree, SSU, Pear Tree
Target100% compliance within six months (a target, not an achieved result)

01

Business problem

Inconsistent or missed lying and standing blood pressure measurements make it harder to identify postural hypotension and to inform appropriate clinical management. This matters for patient safety — particularly falls prevention in older patients.

The initial project assessment identified:

  • Limited consistency in standing blood pressure measurement.
  • Potential gaps in staff awareness and knowledge.
  • No readily available ward-based educational posters, resources or guidance.
  • A need for standardised practice across the participating wards.
  • An opportunity to improve auditability and compliance monitoring.

02

Objective

Improve the consistency and completeness of lying and standing blood pressure measurement across the five participating wards, aiming for 100% compliance with the agreed measurement protocol within six months. The project focused on compliance with the applicable Royal College of Physicians guidance, with standing measurements recorded at the required intervals — particularly 1, 3 and 5 minutes where specified by the project protocol.

03

Approach

Stage 1 · Baseline assessment

  • Review existing blood pressure measurement practice.
  • Conduct a baseline audit using the Nervecentre system.
  • Identify gaps in compliance.

Stage 2 · Staff knowledge assessment

  • Survey nursing staff knowledge of postural hypotension.
  • Identify educational and awareness needs.

Stage 3 · Gap analysis

  • Identify inconsistencies in measurement practice.
  • Recognise the absence of accessible ward-based guidance and posters.
  • Identify opportunities to standardise clinical procedures.

Stage 4 · Proposed improvement interventions

  • Introduce accessible ward-based guidance and posters, where implemented.
  • Support staff education on correct measurement procedures.
  • Reinforce documentation and measurement at the required intervals.
  • Consider repeat audits to assess progress.

Stage 5 · Evaluation and spread

  • Compare subsequent audit results with baseline measurements.
  • Assess progress towards the six-month compliance target.
  • Consider extending to AAU, Walnut and Bay Tree wards and potentially PCH, subject to feasibility and time.

Stage 4 describes proposed interventions; it does not state that every intervention was implemented.

04

Analysis

Compliance was assessed through audit of recorded measurements in Nervecentre and through staff knowledge surveys, then compared against the agreed protocol to identify gaps in practice, awareness and available guidance.

Key performance indicators

  • Percentage of eligible patients receiving the required lying blood pressure measurement.
  • Percentage of eligible patients receiving standing measurements at the required intervals.
  • Overall compliance with the agreed protocol.
  • Nursing staff knowledge or awareness measured through surveys.
  • Completeness of documentation.
  • Change in compliance between baseline and repeat audit.
  • Progress towards the six-month target of 100%.

05

Findings

The initial assessment pointed to inconsistent standing measurements, possible gaps in staff knowledge, no accessible ward-based guidance, and a need for standardised, more auditable practice (see Business problem).

06

Recommendations

  • Standardise the measurement and documentation procedure.
  • Make concise, accessible guidance available on participating wards.
  • Provide staff education and reinforcement.
  • Conduct repeat audits and feed back findings to ward teams.
  • Monitor compliance and identify remaining barriers.
  • Develop a sustainability plan and consider expansion to additional wards.

07

Outcome

The project set a clear compliance target (100% within six months), a stage-based methodology and a KPI framework for monitoring progress. [ADD CURRENT STATUS / RESULTS HERE ONCE CONFIRMED]

08

Business impact

The project aims to improve guideline adherence, support consistent recognition and management of postural hypotension, strengthen clinical documentation and contribute to falls prevention.

Improvements in compliance and patient outcomes must be demonstrated through evaluation rather than assumed.
