DrugStoc — Inventory Forecasting and Stockout Alerts

A business analysis case study on how a pharmaceutical distribution platform could forecast demand and warn pharmacies before they run out of important medicines.

OrganisationDrugStoc
DomainPharmaceutical distribution and supply chain
ContextClasswork task, Healthcare Business Analysis course
FocusStakeholders, requirements, recommended solution and KPIs
ProblemStockouts, overstocking and inefficient procurement
NeedBetter medicine availability and inventory efficiency
Requirements3 functional and 3 business
SolutionForecasting, alerts and a KPI dashboard

01

Company overview

DrugStoc is a technology-driven pharmaceutical distribution company focused on improving access to genuine medicines and strengthening the pharmaceutical supply chain in Africa. Its platform provides healthcare providers with access to medicines, real-time stock visibility, ordering and delivery services.

02

Business problem

Healthcare providers may struggle to maintain optimal medicine inventory while avoiding stockouts, overstocking and inefficient procurement. The problem analysed in this case study is:

How can DrugStoc improve inventory forecasting and proactively alert pharmacies when they are likely to run out of important medicines?

Solving it could help pharmacies reduce stockouts while also reducing unnecessary excess inventory.

03

Stakeholders

Nine stakeholder groups are involved in ordering, holding, supplying, delivering and governing medicines:

  1. Pharmacies
  2. Hospital pharmacies
  3. Pharmacists
  4. DrugStoc procurement team
  5. DrugStoc warehouse / inventory team
  6. Suppliers / manufacturers
  7. Logistics / delivery teams
  8. Customers / patients
  9. Compliance / regulatory teams

04

Business need

To improve medicine availability and inventory efficiency by using historical ordering data, current stock levels and purchasing patterns to identify potential stockouts before they occur.

05

Requirements

Functional requirements

  1. Inventory monitoringThe system should continuously monitor stock levels for medicines purchased by each pharmacy.
  2. Stockout alertsThe system should automatically notify a pharmacy when projected inventory falls below its defined reorder threshold.
  3. Demand forecastingThe system should use historical purchasing data to estimate future medicine demand and recommend appropriate reorder quantities.

Business requirements

  1. Reduce stockoutsReduce the frequency and duration of medicine stockouts among participating pharmacies.
  2. Improve inventory efficiencyReduce excess inventory and associated holding costs.
  3. Improve customer serviceIncrease the availability of commonly requested medicines and improve pharmacy satisfaction.

06

Recommended solution

Develop an AI / data-driven inventory forecasting and replenishment system that follows this flow:

DataOrders, stock levels, purchasing patterns
Demand forecastEstimate future demand
Stockout riskFlag medicines likely to run out
AlertNotify the pharmacy
Recommended orderSuggest reorder quantity
DeliveryFulfil and track the order

A dashboard would also track seven supply-chain and inventory KPIs:

Availability

  • Stockout rate
  • Order fulfilment rate

Inventory efficiency

  • Inventory turnover
  • Average days of inventory

Forecasting

  • Forecast accuracy

Delivery

  • On-time delivery
  • Cancelled orders

07

KPI definitions

Each KPI, how it is calculated and a simple worked example.

KPIDefinitionFormulaSimple example
Stockout ratePercentage of times or items where required stock was unavailable when needed.(Stockout events ÷ Total demand or ordering opportunities) × 10020 stockouts ÷ 500 orders × 100 = 4%
Inventory turnoverHow many times inventory is sold or used and replenished during a period.Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) ÷ Average inventory£500,000 COGS ÷ £100,000 average inventory = 5 times
Forecast accuracyHow close forecasted demand is to actual demand.[1 − |Actual − Forecast| ÷ Actual] × 100Actual 1,000; forecast 900 = 90%
Average days of inventoryHow many days the current inventory will cover, based on consumption or sales.(Average inventory ÷ COGS) × Number of days£100k ÷ £500k × 365 = 73 days
Order fulfilment ratePercentage of customer orders fulfilled, usually completely and correctly.(Orders fulfilled ÷ Total orders) × 100950 ÷ 1,000 × 100 = 95%
On-time deliveryPercentage of orders delivered by the promised date or time.(Orders delivered on time ÷ Total delivered orders) × 100920 ÷ 1,000 × 100 = 92%
Cancelled ordersNumber or percentage of orders cancelled before fulfilment.(Cancelled orders ÷ Total orders) × 10030 ÷ 1,000 × 100 = 3%

Examples use illustrative figures to show the calculation; they are not DrugStoc results.
